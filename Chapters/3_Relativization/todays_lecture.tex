\section{Gödel's Constructible Universe}

Define the class function $A \mapsto \D(A)$ by
\begin{align*}
    \D(A) = \setst{X \ssq A}{X \text{ is definable with parameters from $A$ over the structure $(A, \in)$}}
\end{align*}

% Transcribed from the board photographs supplied with the post-lecture pass of 25 Oct 2026, as were the other passages below carrying this comment; these boards were not typed up during the lecture.

Ie, $X \in \D(A)$ iff there are $\bar{a} \in A^{<\omega}$ and $n < \omega$ such that, letting $\vp$ be the $n$th formula, we have that
\begin{align*}
    X = \setst{b \in A}{(A, \in) \models \vp\of{b, \bar{a}}}
\end{align*}

Recall that $(A, \in) \models \vp\of{b, \bar{a}}$ can be written $A \models \vp\of{b, \bar{a}}$, and that this holds iff $\vp\of{b, \bar{a}}^A$.

We don't need the Power Set Axiom to know that $\D(A)$ exists for all $A$, ie, $\ZFwoP$ is enough. Use Replacement to know that $\D(A)$ is the range of the class function
\begin{align*}
    \parenth{\bar{a}, n} \mapsto \text{the subset of $A$ defined by the $n$th formula and parameters $\bar{a}$}
\end{align*}
Its domain is contained in $A^{<\omega} \times \omega$, so it's a set. By Replacement, we know the range is a set.

\begin{boxdefinition}[The $L$-Hierarchy]
    Define $L_0 = 0$, $L_{\alpha + 1} = \D\of{L_{\alpha}}$ for all ordinals $\alpha$, and for all limit ordinals $\beta$, $L_{\beta} = \bigcup_{\alpha < \beta} L_{\alpha}$. Then define the universe $L = \bigcup_{\alpha \in \OR} L_{\alpha}$.
\end{boxdefinition}

% Transcribed from the board photographs.

One big goal (Gödel): working in $\ZF$, show that $L$ is a transitive class model of $\ZFC + \GCH +$ lots of other stuff. Also, if $M$ is any transitive proper class model of $\ZF$, then $L = L^M \ssq M$.

Intuition: in the $V$-hierarchy, we jump from the countable $V_{\omega} = \HF$ to the uncountable $V_{\omega + 1} = \Powset\of{V_{\omega}}$ in one step. Also, assuming $\AC$ in $V$, $\abs{V_{\omega + 1}} = 2^{\aleph_0}$, and we can't tell how big $2^{\aleph_0}$ is!

We'll see that $L_{\omega} = \HF = V_{\omega}$, but $L_{\omega + 1} = \D\of{L_{\omega}}$ is also countable. Why? Count formulae and parameters from $L_{\omega}$. By using $\D$ instead of $\Powset$, we slow things way down: there's hope of understanding and answering questions.

We can show that $\grpres{L_{\alpha}}{\alpha \in \OR}$ is a hierarchy of sets, ie, $L_{\alpha} \subset L_{\beta}$ for $\alpha < \beta$, and each $L_{\alpha}$ is a transitive set. We can also check that $L_{\alpha} \cap \OR = \alpha$.

\begin{boxlemma}[Properties of the Class Function $A \mapsto \D(A)$] \hfill
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item $A \mapsto \D\of{A}$ is defined in $\ZFwoP$.
        \item $A \mapsto \D\of{A}$ is absolute for transitive class models of $\ZFwoP$.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{boxlemma}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
    We defined formulae to be certain members of $\HF$: ``$\vp$ is a formula'' is absolute.

    % Transcribed from the board photographs.
    We defined $A \models \vp\of{\bar{a}}$, for a formula $\vp$ and $\bar{a} \in A^{<\omega}$, by recursion on the complexity of $\vp$. This is absolute.

    Also note that $A \models \exists a_1 \, \forall a_2 \, \exists a_3 \cdots Q a_n \, \psi$, with $\psi$ quantifier-free, is equivalent to the $\Delta_0$ formula
    \begin{align*}
        \exists a_1 \in A \, \forall a_2 \in A \, \exists a_3 \in A \cdots Q a_n \in A \, \psi
    \end{align*}
    The absoluteness of the recursive definition says more than this: it is the class function $\parenth{A, \vp, \bar{a}} \mapsto \set{0, 1}$ that is absolute.
\end{proof}

\begin{boxlemma}
    If $A$ is finite, then $\D(A) = \Powset(A)$.
\end{boxlemma}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
    Just unfold the definitions.
\end{proof}

\begin{boxcorollary}
    For all $n \leq \omega$, $L_n = V_n$.
\end{boxcorollary}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
    Consider $n = 0$, $n$ successor and $n = \omega$, in all cases we're ok.
\end{proof}

Let's now talk briefly about cardinality. Note that we will not assume choice here, so in particular, we cannot conclude every set has a cardinality. So we need to think about things in terms of well-orderings.

\begin{boxlemma}
    If $A$ has a cardinality and it's infinite, then $\D(A)$ has a cardinality and $\abs{\D(A)} = \abs{A}$.
\end{boxlemma}
\begin{proof}
    Say $\abs{A} = \alpha$. Notice that
    \begin{align*}
        \abs{\D(A)} \leq \abs{\underbrace{\text{Formulae}}_{\text{countable}} \times A^{<\omega}} = \abs{A}
    \end{align*}
\end{proof}

\begin{boxcorollary}
    Each $L_{\alpha}$ has a cardinality and, for $\alpha \geq \omega$, $\abs{L_{\alpha}} = \abs{\alpha}$.
\end{boxcorollary}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
    Argue by recursion on $\alpha \in \OR$. Base and successor cases are not hard. For the limit case, just end-extend all well-orderings $L_{\alpha}$ for $\alpha < \beta$, $\beta$ being some limit ordinal. This gives a well-ordering.
\end{proof}

With this we end our discussion of cardinality.

\begin{boxlemma}
    For all $\alpha \in \OR$, $L_{\alpha} \ssq V_{\alpha}$.
\end{boxlemma}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
    $\D(A) \ssq \Powset(A)$.
\end{proof}

We now come to the most important theorem about $L$. We work in $\ZF$.

\begin{boxtheorem}[Gödel]
    For each axiom $\sigma$ of $\ZFC \cup \GCH$, we have $\sigma^{L}$.
\end{boxtheorem}
\begin{proof}
    \hfill
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \underline{Extensionality.} $L$ is transitive.

        \item \underline{Pairing.} Let $x, y \in L$. Pick $\alpha \in \OR$ such that $x, y \in L_{\alpha}$. Then $\set{x, y} \in \D\of{L_{\alpha}} = L_{\alpha + 1} \subset L$.

        \item \underline{Comprehension.} Let $A \in L$, $\bar{y} \in L$, $B = \setst{x \in A}{\vp^L\of{x, \bar{y}}}$. We need $B \in L$.

        Apply the Reflection Theorem Scheme to the $L$-hierarchy to find $\alpha$ such that $\vp$ is absolute between $L_{\alpha}$ and $L$. We can do this with $A \in L_{\alpha}$ and $\bar{y} \in L_{\alpha}$.

        Then, since $A \ssq L_{\alpha}$,
        \begin{align*}
            B
            = \setst{x \in L_{\alpha}}{L_{\alpha} \models \parenth{x \in A \land \vp\of{x, \bar{y}}}}
            \in \D\of{L_{\alpha}} = L_{\alpha + 1} \subset L
        \end{align*}

        \item \underline{Unions.} Let Let $\mathcal{F} \in L$. Pick $\alpha$ such that $\mathcal{F} \in L_{\alpha}$. Then,
        % [FILLED] the set-builder and the justification after it; the lecture left both sides of the set-builder blank
        \begin{align*}
            \bigcup \mathcal{F}
            = \setst{x \in L_{\alpha}}{L_{\alpha} \models \exists y \, \parenth{y \in \mathcal{F} \land x \in y}}
            \in \D\of{L_{\alpha}} = L_{\alpha + 1} \subset L
        \end{align*}
        Indeed, $L_{\alpha}$ is transitive, so every $y \in \mathcal{F}$ lies in $L_{\alpha}$, and then so does every $x \in y$. So $\bigcup \mathcal{F} \ssq L_{\alpha}$, and for $x \in L_{\alpha}$, $L_{\alpha} \models \exists y \, \parenth{y \in \mathcal{F} \land x \in y}$ iff some $y \in \mathcal{F}$ has $x \in y$. In particular $\bigcup \mathcal{F}$ is a member of $L$ containing every member of $\mathcal{F}$ as a subset, which is what the Axiom of Unions asks of $L$.

        % Transcribed from the board photographs.
        \item \underline{Power Set.} Let $A \in L$. We need $\Powset(A) \cap L \in L$. It is enough to show that there is some $B \in L$ with $\Powset(A) \cap L \ssq B$.

        In $V$, consider the class function $X \mapsto$ the least $\xi$ such that $X \in L_{\xi}$, with domain $\Powset(A) \cap L$. By Replacement in $V$ (plus stuff), we know there's some $\alpha$ such that $\forall X \in \Powset(A) \cap L \, \parenth{X \in L_{\alpha}}$. Take $B = L_{\alpha} \in L$; this works.

        \item \underline{Replacement.} Assume $A, \bar{z} \in L$ and $\forall x \in A \, \exists! y \in L \, \vp^L\of{x, y, \bar{z}}$. Pick $\alpha$ such that $A, \bar{z} \in L_{\alpha}$. Consider the class function
        \begin{align*}
            x \mapsto \text{the least $\xi$ such that } \exists y \in L_{\xi} \, \vp^L\of{x, y, \bar{z}}
        \end{align*}
        By Replacement in $V$, there's some $\beta$ such that all such $\xi$s are $< \beta$. Then $L_{\beta} \in L$ and $\forall x \in A \, \exists y \in L_{\beta} \, \vp^L\of{x, y, \bar{z}}$.

        \item \underline{Foundation.} $L \ssq V = \WF$.

        \item \underline{Infinity.} $\omega \in L$.
    \end{itemize}
\end{proof}

So now we know $L$ models $\ZF$. By the absoluteness of $A \mapsto \D(A)$ and $\alpha \mapsto L_{\alpha}$, $L^L = L$, and $L^M = L_{\OR \cap M}$ for every transitive class model $M$ of $\ZFwoP$.

We said that the class fuctions $A \mapsto \D(A)$ and $\alpha \mapsto L_{\alpha}$ are absolute for transitive class models of $\ZFwoP$.

This implies that if $M$ is such a model, then either $\OR \cap M = \OR$ and $L^M = L$ or $\OR \cap M = \alpha \in \OR$ and $L^M = L_{\alpha}$.

In fact, there is a finite subtheory of $\ZFwoP$ that's enough for this.

We indicated how to define a well-ordering $<_{\alpha}$ of $L_{\alpha}$ by recursion on $\alpha \in \OR$:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $<_0 = \emptyset$
    \item For $x, y \in L_{\alpha + 1} = \D\of{L_{\alpha}}$, put $x <_{\alpha + 1} y$ iff one of the following occurs:
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item $x, y \in L_{\alpha}$ and $x <_{\alpha} y$
        \item $x \in L_{\alpha}$ and $y \in L_{\alpha + 1} \setminus L_{\alpha}$
        \item $x, y \in L_{\alpha + 1} \setminus L_{\alpha}$ and $(k, p) <_{\lex} (l, q)$, where $(k, p)$ is the lexicographically least pair in $\omega \times L_{\alpha}^{< \omega}$ such that if $\vp$ is the $k$th formula, then $x = \setst{z \in L_{\alpha}}{L_{\alpha} \models \vp(z, p)}$, and $(l, q)$ is the analogous thing for $y$
    \end{enumerate}
    Note that $<_{\alpha + 1}$ end-extends $<_{\alpha}$.
    
    \item For limit ordinals $\beta$, put
    \begin{align*}
        <_{\beta} = \bigcup_{\alpha < \beta} <_{\alpha}
    \end{align*}
\end{itemize}
This works: $\alpha \mapsto <_{\alpha}$ is definable without parameters; moreover, it is absolute for transitive models of a certain finite subtheory of $\ZFwoP$. We fix a name for it.

\begin{boxnotation}
    For the rest of this section, $T$ will denote a finite subtheory of $\ZFwoP$ such that $\alpha \mapsto L_{\alpha}$ and $\alpha \mapsto <_{\alpha}$ are both absolute for transitive models of $T$.
\end{boxnotation}

Notice that if we put
\begin{align*}
    <_{L} = \bigcup_{\alpha \in \OR} <_{\alpha}
\end{align*}
then $<_L$ is a class well-ordering of $L$. Note that it is set-like, extensional and well-founded, so it admits a Mostowski Collapse. This Mostowski Collapse turns out to simply be $\OR$.

For any transitive class model $M$ of $T$ ($T$ being the finite subtheory of $\ZFwoP$ that gives us the $<_{\alpha}$s),
\begin{align*}
    \parenth{<_L}^M
    = \begin{cases}
        <_L & \text{ if } \OR \cap M = \OR \\
        <_{\alpha} & \text{ if } \OR \cap M = \alpha
    \end{cases}
\end{align*}

\begin{boxtheorem}[using $\ZF$ in $V$]
    $(\AC)^L$
\end{boxtheorem}
\begin{proof}
    % Transcribed from the board photographs.
    Let $A \in L$. Pick $\alpha$ such that $A \in L_{\alpha}$. Then $A \ssq L_{\alpha}$. Let $R = <_{\alpha} \cap \parenth{A \times A}$. Since $\parenth{<_{\alpha}}^L = <_{\alpha}$, we know $R \in L$. We know $(A, R)$ is a well-ordering. This is $\Pi_1$, hence downward absolute to $L$.
\end{proof}

The well-ordering $<_L$ also tells us that every constructible set can be defined from an ordinal. To say this, we need the classes of sets definable from ordinals. We cannot just say ``$x$ is definable from finitely many ordinals'', as there is no formula expressing definability (cf. \Cref{Ch3:Sec:Satisfaction}); so we ask for a definition over some $V_{\alpha}$ instead.

\begin{boxdefinition}[Ordinal Definability]
    A set $x$ is \textbf{ordinal definable} iff there are an ordinal $\alpha$ with $x \in V_{\alpha}$, a formula $\vp\of{u, \bar{v}}$ and ordinals $\bar{\xi} \in \alpha^{<\omega}$ such that $x$ is the unique $y \in V_{\alpha}$ with $V_{\alpha} \models \vp\of{y, \bar{\xi}}$. We write $\OD$ for the class of ordinal definable sets.

    A set $x$ is \textbf{hereditarily ordinal definable} iff $\trcl{\set{x}} \ssq \OD$. We write $\HOD$ for the class of hereditarily ordinal definable sets.
\end{boxdefinition}

By the Reflection Theorem Scheme, this is what we wanted: for each formula $\vp\of{u, \bar{v}}$, if $\bar{\xi}$ are ordinals and $x$ is the unique set with $\vp\of{x, \bar{\xi}}$, then reflecting $\vp$ to a level $V_{\alpha}$ with $x, \bar{\xi} \in V_{\alpha}$ shows $x \in \OD$. Conversely, a set in $\OD$ is defined in $V$ from the ordinals $\alpha$ and $\bar{\xi}$ by the formula ``$u \in V_{\alpha}$ and $V_{\alpha} \models \vp\of{u, \bar{\xi}}$''.

\begin{boxtheorem}
    $L \ssq \HOD$, and $\HOD^L = L$.
\end{boxtheorem}
\begin{proof}
    Note that given any $x \in L$, we have some $\xi \in \OR$ such that $x$ is the $\xi$th member of $L$ according to $<_L$. In other words, if $G : (L, <_L) \simeq \OR$ then $G(x) = \xi$. Thus, $x$ is definable from $\xi$, so $x \in \OD$. Since $L$ is transitive, $x \in \HOD$. This calculation is absolute down to $L$. So $\HOD^L = L$.
\end{proof}

\begin{boxtheorem}[Working in $L$]
    $(\GCH)^L$. Ie,
    \begin{align*}
        \parenth{\text{For all infinite cardinals $\kappa$, } \kappa^+ = 2^{\kappa}}^L
    \end{align*}
\end{boxtheorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof, working in $L$ and keeping in mind that $L^L = L$]
    Let $\lambda$ be an infinite cardinal. We will show that $H_{\lambda} = L_{\lambda}$. This will be enough because if we have any infinite cardinal $\kappa$, setting $\lambda = \kappa^+$, we get that
    \begin{align*}
        \Powset(\kappa) \ssq H_{\lambda} = L_{\lambda}
    \end{align*}
    meaning that
    \begin{align*}
        2^{\kappa} = \abs{\Powset(\kappa)} \leq \abs{L_{\lambda}} = \abs{\lambda} = \lambda = \kappa^+
    \end{align*}

    And of course the other inequality is always true in $L$, as in any model of $\ZFC$.

    So now let's prove that $H_{\lambda} = L_{\lambda}$ for all infinite cardinals $\lambda$.

    Fix an infinite cardinal $\lambda$. We already know that
    \begin{align*}
        H_{\omega} = V_{\omega} = L_{\omega}
    \end{align*}
    so we may assume that $\lambda$ is uncountable.
    \begin{itemize}
        % [CORRECTED] was: ``Pick $\alpha < \lambda$ such that $z \in L_{\alpha}$'' --- $\abs{L_{\alpha}} = \abs{\alpha}$ fails for finite $\alpha \geq 3$ (eg $\abs{L_3} = 4$); as $\lambda > \omega$ we may take $\alpha \geq \omega$
        \item \underline{$L_{\lambda} \ssq H_{\lambda}$.} Let $z \in L_{\lambda}$. Pick $\alpha$ with $\omega \leq \alpha < \lambda$ such that $z \in L_{\alpha}$. Then certainly $\trcl{\set{z}} \ssq L_{\alpha}$. So
        \begin{align*}
            \abs{\trcl{\set{z}}} \leq \abs{L_{\alpha}} = \abs{\alpha}\leq \alpha < \lambda
        \end{align*}
        So $z \in H_{\lambda}$.

        \item \underline{$H_{\lambda} \ssq L_{\lambda}$.} Let $z \in H_{\lambda}$. Recall our finite subtheory $T$ of $\ZFwoP$, for whose transitive models $\alpha \mapsto L_{\alpha}$ and $\alpha \mapsto <_{\alpha}$ are absolute, maybe augmented with $V = L$.
        By the reflection theorem scheme, there's some $\beta$ such that $z \in L_{\beta}$ and $\sigma^{L_{\beta}}$ for all $\sigma \in T$ and $(V = L)^{L_{\beta}}$. So $L_{\beta} \models T$ and $(V = L)^{L_{\beta}}$.

        Observe: $\trcl{\set{z}} \ssq L_{\beta}$ and $\abs{\trcl{\set{z}}} < \lambda$. By Downwards Löwenheim-Skolem, we have some $X \preceq L_{\beta}$ with $\abs{X} < \lambda$ and $\trcl{\set{z}} \ssq X$. Let $G : X \simeq M$ be the Mostowski Collapse of $X$, so $M$ is a transitive model of $T$. Since $\trcl{\set{z}} \ssq X$, we know that
        \begin{align*}
            G \restriction \trcl{\set{z}} = \id \restriction \trcl{\set{z}}
        \end{align*}
        In particular, $G(z) = z \in M$.

        As $L_{\beta} \models V = L$, we also have $M \models V = L$. In other words, $(V = L)^M$. This says that $V^M = L^M$, which says $M = L_{\alpha}$ where $\alpha = \OR \cap M$. Therefore, $z \in L_{\alpha}$ and $\abs{X} = \abs{M} = \abs{L_{\alpha}} = \abs{\alpha}$, and this is less than $\lambda$, so $\alpha < \lambda$. 
    \end{itemize}
\end{proof}

We do something very interesting in the above proof: we take some nice level of the $L$ hierarchy (which we get by the reflection theorem scheme), we take an elementary substructure (which we get by downwards Löwenheim-Skolem), then take its Mostowski collapse, and we end up with \textit{another} level of the $L$ hierarchy.

It turns out that this is not true of the $V$- or the $H$-hierarchy. The phenomenon has a name.

\begin{boxdefinition}[Condensation]
    The phenomenon that the Mostowski collapse of an elementary substructure of a suitable level of the $L$-hierarchy---such as the $L_{\beta}$ in the proof above, with $L_{\beta} \models T$ and $(V = L)^{L_{\beta}}$---is again a level of the $L$-hierarchy is known as \textbf{condensation}.
\end{boxdefinition}
